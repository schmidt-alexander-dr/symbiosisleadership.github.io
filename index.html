Where you are

The moment that progress stalls

The easy wins are done, and you did them well. Targets are in place and dashboards full of data. Neither is telling you what to do next. Meanwhile your CFO wants the business case in one number before budget season, red pencil already out over next year's headcount. There is no one-number answer you can give.

Your industry is stuck in a prisoner's dilemma. Every next step looks unreasonable if you take it alone. Cut one color from next season's line and you cut overproduction, but also your sales. Your CSO is pleased. Your investors and your customers are not.

Your platform, standard, or protocol can make a difference, and you want it to matter beyond your own P&L. Building it is the easy part. The hard part is who needs to be around the table to test it, break it, and launch it with you.

Every next announcement feels like a trap. Publishing progress invites scrutiny, publishing a miss invites schadenfreude. So the incentive is to game the number or quietly lower the ambition. You can see the race to the bottom. You can't see the way out.

How I work

This is where our work begins

The part that usually surprises leaders when we first speak is what I don't do. So let me say it upfront: I won't tell you how to solve it.

In my experience the person with the problem already holds most of the answer, but it's buried under time pressure, obscured by incomplete information, and seemingly inaccessible when viewed from within.

My work is to create the conditions under which the answer surfaces. It goes beyond listening and asking what you think you should do. It's systems thinking that turns reflection into a decision you can act on.

I bring my own material to that process: applied work in carbon accounting, supply chain ESG traceability, climate finance, and standards governance. When your problem is technical I can go deep with you. When it hinges on who else needs to be in the room, I usually know them. But always the counsel comes first.

Two more things I don't do, besides presenting you a solution in a flashy slide deck: acting as the extended sales arm for a single solution provider, and project management that someone else could do just as well.

Symbiotic Leadership

Why this works, and why it's different

Symbiotic Leadership means recognizing that none of us will be well if the planet is not. It's a shift from extractive to generative, in how we think, lead, and create value.

It is not a call to renounce ambition. It is a return to the original meaning of competition: com-petere, to strive together. The change starts ground-up, with leaders who have the humility to admit they don't have all the answers alone, and the courage to find them together.

That's also why the four situations above tend to resist ordinary consulting. Each one is a coordination problem disguised as a strategy problem.
